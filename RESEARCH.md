# Message Not Sent: research notes

What makes narrative chat/text games emotional and replayable. Sources: Steam/Wikipedia (Emily Is Away), anormallostphone.com, App Store (Lifeline), Mechanics of Magic critiques (Florence 2026, "Unsent Drafts" 2025, "Unsaid" 2025), The Unsent Project coverage, and the itch.io "unsent" release thread.

## Reference games
- **Emily Is Away (and Too):** the whole game is a period-accurate IM client. You pick a reply and it *types itself out* letter by letter, and sometimes the game rewrites what you meant into something more guarded. The emotional punch comes from the gap between what you wanted to say and what gets sent. It covers years in short chapters (time skips), and endings come from choices that build up over the game.
- **A Normal Lost Phone:** a fake phone OS, where meaning is spread across messages, photos and apps. It handles difficult topics with empathy and treats intimacy and privacy as the theme. Its key idea is "if you close the app but still think about the game, are you still playing?" Realism in the UI does a lot of the storytelling.
- **Lifeline:** real-time notifications, with a character who depends on you. Waiting and typing pauses build tension, and rewind/replay makes it easy to try other branches.
- **Florence:** almost no dialogue. Conversation is a jigsaw that gets easier as the relationship grows and jagged during arguments, so the interaction itself carries the feeling. It's short (around 30 to 40 minutes), built on everyday routines, and ends with you growing on your own after the relationship.
- **Choices / Episode-style stories:** chapter structure, a clear "your choices matter" signal, cliffhangers at chapter ends, chapter select and replay, and endings you can collect.
- **"Unsent" trend:** The Unsent Project has 5M+ anonymous messages people never sent. Its premise is that writing the message is the catharsis, and sending it is optional. Indie games like *Unsent Drafts* (Send / Edit / Delete choices that add up to one of 3 endings) and *unsent* (hesitation as the only mechanic) show that **deleting can be a meaningful choice**.

## Design takeaways used in this game
1. **Type, delete, rewrite, send:** drafts type themselves into a realistic composer. You can backspace them away (they go into an "unsent" archive you can read later) and pick again. Not sending is always an option.
2. **Branch and bottleneck:** many local branches merge back at chapter beats. Hidden "closure / friendship / self / self-love" tones decide which final messages you're *ready* to write. Locked options say "not ready yet", which makes you want to replay.
3. **Phone realism:** typing indicators that start and stop, Delivered/Read receipts, late-night timestamps, notification banners, photos, voice notes, group chats.
4. **Time skips:** Day 1, then Day 3, Week 3, Month 3. The UI moves from soft daylight gradients into night mode as the hours get later.
5. **Healthy tone:** raw 2 AM messages can be typed, and the game gently asks "send it now, or sleep on it?" They're never rewarded. Friends and family model good support, and short wellbeing tips appear. Teen-safe, with no explicit content.
6. **Replayability:** 5 endings in a gallery, chapter select that remembers your tone at each chapter, an unsent-drafts archive, and counts of messages typed, deleted and sent at the end.
